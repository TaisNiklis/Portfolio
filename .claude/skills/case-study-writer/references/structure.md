# Case study structure

Total: 800–1500 words, 8–15 images, 4–6 minutes to read.
Every section heading must make sense on its own, so the case can be skimmed by headings.

## 0. Header card (above the fold)
- **Title = outcome**, not a project name.
  - ✅ "Making AI features people actually use: +X% adoption in a B2B SaaS"
  - ✅ "Untangling 7 user roles into one clear permission model"
  - ❌ "AI Chat Redesign", "Project for Client X"
- Meta: Product · Role · Team · Timeline · Platform · My contribution.
- 2–3 key metrics as big numbers, each with a short label.
- Hero image: the best screen, in context.

## 1. TL;DR (3–4 sentences)
Problem → what I did → result.
> Users of [product] ignored its AI assistant: only X% tried it. I audited the experience,
> interviewed N users and redesigned onboarding, quick replies and in-flow hints. AI usage
> grew by Y% in Z weeks.

## 2. Context (~100 words)
What the product is, who uses it, the business model, constraints (deadline, budget, legacy,
tech stack, NDA).

## 3. Problem (~100 words)
The business problem and the user problem, each backed by evidence (data, interviews, audit).
End with one line: **"How might we …?"**

## 4. Goals & success metrics
2–4 bullets: what success meant and how it was measured.

## 5. My role
What I owned and what the team did. Who I worked with (PM, devs, client, other designers).
How many designers I coordinated. Keep it to 3–5 bullets.

## 6. Discovery & insights
Methods used (interviews, stakeholder sessions, UX audit, analytics, competitor review).
**3–5 insights**, each in the form *insight → evidence → design implication*.

## 7. Exploration & decisions (the senior section)
- 2–3 options considered, shown as sketches or wireframes, and why the others were dropped.
- Trade-offs: scope, tech limits, time.
- A moment where I changed the client's or team's mind, and how.
Phrase as decisions: "I chose X over Y because …".

## 8. Solution
3–5 key decisions. Each: a screen or flow plus an annotation of *what changed and why*.
Before/after where possible. Don't add screens that don't support a decision.

## 9. Delivery
Handoff (screen maps, annotations), design system components used or created, work with
devs, QA, edge cases.

## 10. Impact
Metrics with their source, client or user feedback (a quote if possible), and what changed
in the team's process.

## 11. Learnings & next steps
1–3 honest points: what I'd do differently, what I'd test next.

## 12. AI in my process (short callout)
Where Claude / Figma AI helped (research synthesis, audit, copy, flows), and which decisions
stayed human. 2–4 lines.
