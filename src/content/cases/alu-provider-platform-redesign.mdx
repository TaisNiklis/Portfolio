## TL;DR
ALU connects customers with beauty and healthcare providers. Its provider web platform **looked inconsistent and had to work for very different businesses**: a freelancer, a salon and a clinic chain with several branches. The client had a limited budget and wanted to keep the existing functionality. I audited the platform, agreed a focused first phase with the client, and delivered **a new visual direction, a design system and six core flows**. Broader UX changes went into a documented backlog. The design was handed to development and is partially implemented.

## Context
ALU has two products: a **mobile app** where customers discover and book services, and a **web platform** where providers run their business. Providers manage services, specialists, branches and their business profile.

Constraints:
- **A limited client budget.** We could not fix everything the audit found.
- **Functionality stays as it is.** The redesign changes how the product looks and is organised, not what it does.

## The problem
**Business problem:** the provider platform is the supply side of the marketplace. If providers find it hard to set up services, specialists and branches, customers in the app see fewer and weaker listings. [TODO: did the client name a business reason — new providers, complaints, sales?]

**User problem:** the interface **looked like a low-fidelity prototype**: shades of grey, generic icons, bold text everywhere. Similar tasks worked differently across sections: adding a service opened a new page, required-field asterisks appeared in one module only, and labels changed between the menu and the page ("Appointments" vs. "Schedule"). Some steps were dead ends, such as a verification screen with no "Back" and no "Resend code".

**How might we give every type of provider one clear way to manage their business, within a budget that allows only a first phase?**

## Discovery & insights
The audit was a **heuristic evaluation of the full provider journey**: sign-up, dashboard, profile, appointments, customers, services, specialists and branches. Each finding has a problem, a recommendation and a summary, and **each section ends with a priority matrix: critical, high, medium / low**. The result was a 62-page report with 53 findings. [TODO: did I run the audit alone or with other designers?]

<Figure src="[TODO: audit-findings.png]" caption="One audit finding and a section priority matrix. The priorities became the basis for the phase-one scope." />

1. **There was no shared visual system.** Text contrast was as low as **1.08:1** on the verification field and **2.29–2.81:1** on labels and inputs, against the 4.5:1 WCAG minimum. The CTA colour was used for selected options and secondary elements, so the main action did not stand out. → Build the palette, type scale and component states first, then apply them to every flow.
2. **The same task worked differently in each section.** Services and specialists opened full pages for adding items, the service form had its own image uploader, and the language switcher appeared twice. → Reuse one set of patterns, such as the side drawer, across all management areas.
3. **The structure did not support growth.** A specialist could not upgrade to a salon account and had to start a new one, **losing reviews, services and clients**. Business types at sign-up had no descriptions. → Design one entity model where a freelancer can grow into a salon or a chain.
4. **New providers started from an empty screen.** The setup showed 3 steps but opened the product after step 1. The dashboard, customers and branches pages had no first action, and the branches page showed filters before any branch existed. → Every empty state explains the next step.
5. **Entities were shown in isolation.** A specialist profile listed every salon service, not the ones they perform. Branches could only be compared through filters. → Show relationships: specialist ↔ services, branch ↔ specialists.

## Exploring options
### Focused phase one instead of a full redesign
53 findings were more than the budget could cover, and many of them, such as notifications, financial widgets or a weekly calendar view, meant new functionality. **I chose a focused first phase over a full UX overhaul**: a new visual layer, a design system and the core flows, without new features. The functional recommendations stayed in the report as **a ready backlog for future iterations**.
