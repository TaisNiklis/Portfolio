## TL;DR
An AI advertising platform covered the full campaign cycle, but **users saw a set of separate tools, not one product**. Reports sat in the main menu and again inside campaigns, "Create campaign" sent people to a planning tool they didn't expect, and the AI assistant went unnoticed. As UX lead, I ran the audit and 9 interviews, then rebuilt the product around **one Project entity, one guided campaign flow and an AI assistant built into the work**. We shipped it to the client's developers in agile batches before a planned product presentation.

## Context
The platform helps brands research audiences and competitors, plan media, launch campaigns and track performance. It serves three role groups: brand clients, internal managers and admins.

I worked on the agency side as UX lead. I owned research, IA, flows and the client presentations.

Constraints:
- **A full redesign close to the client's deadline.** Developers worked in parallel, so critical pages went to dev as soon as they were ready.
- A design system inherited from a previous agency, and a brand refresh happening at the same time.
- **Client role first.** Manager and admin flows were deferred.

*Client name, people and data are hidden under NDA.*

## The problem
**Business problem:** the client wanted a **full-cycle platform** for research, planning, launch and optimisation. But the product looked like a tool for submitting campaigns, with research "on the side". In their VP of Product's words, they needed "a platform for researching, planning and creating campaigns".

**User problem:** users understood tools one by one but not the lifecycle. Navigation changed with the selected company or report. Terminology was inconsistent. Many users didn't know what the AI chat could do.

**How might we turn separate tools into one campaign workflow, with AI help where users already work?**

## Goals
- One entity and one flow from brief to reporting.
- Clear navigation: where users *do* things vs. where they *see* results.
- Turn the homepage into both a daily dashboard and a showcase of the full cycle.
- A consistent design system, so the product stops being built page by page.
- Main success signal: **by the end of the first month, a new client checks the platform daily instead of waiting for an email report.** [TODO: was this tracked after launch?]

## Discovery & insights
I started with an audit of heuristics, consistency, analytics, flows and loading performance. I collected the findings into one prioritised report. Then I mapped departments to modules and lifecycle stages from the interviews.

<Figure src="[TODO: audit-report.png]" caption="UX audit: screen-level issues grouped by heuristic and priority. The duplicated reports in the menu became the main structural finding." />

1. **Users saw tools, not a process.** In interviews, users explained each tool but not the lifecycle. → Build the product around a campaign entity, not tool sections.
2. **Reports lived in two places.** The audit found reports both in the main menu and inside campaigns. → Nest every report under its project.
3. **"Create campaign" broke users' mental model.** It redirected to "Advanced Planning", and a client reviewer reported the same confusion. → Make planning a step inside creation.
4. **An empty dashboard looks broken.** Interviews showed that data arrives on day 3 and is complete by day 7. → Onboarding explains the timeline and never shows an empty screen.
5. **AI value was invisible.** Users didn't know what the chat could do. → Put AI inside tasks, with ready-made actions.

## Exploring options
### One Project entity instead of separate features
I proposed a **Project** (formerly "Campaign") that exists **from the brief onward**. It holds the media plan, research, reporting dashboard, activity log and files. I chose "Project" over "Campaign" because the entity has to exist before a campaign goes live. The client approved this as the base structure.

<Figure src="[TODO: entity-tree.png]" caption="Entity tree: staff → clients → projects. Each project holds its plan, research, reports, log and files." />

### One status, many views
The status map covers **12 statuses and 8 roles**. A project has one real status, but each role sees its own label. A client never sees an internal draft, and sees **"Needs Revision" instead of "Rejected"** to protect the relationship.

<Figure src="[TODO: status-map.png]" caption="Status map: 12 statuses × 8 roles. One real state, different views per role." />

### Two modes, not a flat menu
The client's team proposed a flat menu: Campaigns, Research, Plan and AI at the same level, like SEMrush. **I argued against it.** In a flat menu, clients can't tell where they *do* things from where they only *see* reports. The VP of Product agreed. **We kept two modes, Projects and Tools.** Tools results can be saved into a project or into a client-level Brand Hub. The client locked the decision once the homepage took over the "full-cycle showcase" job.

### Light sidebar over dark rail
I presented two layouts: a dark icon rail and a light sidebar with a top bar. The commercial lead found the dark rail too heavy and liked search in the header. **I reframed the choice.** We needed a clear hierarchy, not a dark colour. The merged option is a **light, collapsible sidebar with search and profile in the header**. It met every point of feedback without an either/or.

## The solution
### 1. One button, one guided flow
**"Create New Campaign"** lives on the homepage and the Campaigns page. Advanced Planning is no longer a separate tool; it is step 1. **Research is optional.** Users can attach saved research or click "Continue without research". A campaign can't exist without a media plan, but it can exist without research. Unfinished plans save as drafts.

<Figure src="[TODO: campaign-flow-before-after.png]" caption="Before: 'Create campaign' redirected to a separate planning tool. After: one guided flow with an optional research step." />

### 2. An AI assistant inside the work
The assistant became a **persistent element in the bottom-right corner, not a menu item**. It has contextual entry points, a side panel that expands to full screen, and AI insights on the homepage. After client review, we replaced the "Try asking" prompts with **quick-action tags that pre-fill an editable prompt**. We also moved 6 specialised agents into a dropdown inside the input field.

<Figure src="[TODO: ai-before-after.png]" caption="AI assistant before / after: a hidden chat vs. quick actions, an agent picker and contextual entry points." />

<Figure src="[TODO: ai-layouts.png]" caption="Side panel and full workspace: quick questions stay next to the task, longer analysis gets full space." />

### 3. A homepage with two jobs
The homepage is now a **daily dashboard and a full-cycle showcase**. It holds the hero, the Brand Hub, the latest campaigns, tool widgets, AI insights and AI Labs. **Premium modules stay visible but locked, with a "Contact Us" button.** Upsell goes through sales reps, never automatically. ("Gate, don't hide.")

<Figure src="[TODO: homepage-before-after.png]" caption="Homepage before / after: from low-value overview to live data, saved research and AI insights." />

### 4. Onboarding that never starts empty
New clients arrive with one active campaign. Onboarding combines a short tour, a knowledge base with videos, contextual AI tips and a **day 3 / day 7 data timeline**. On day 3, the assistant offers to explain the first numbers.

<Figure src="[TODO: onboarding.png]" caption="Onboarding: tour, data timeline and the first AI explanation on day 3." />

### 5. Design system and brand
The audit found **contrast problems, an undefined palette structure and too few type styles**. I proposed a palette template that defines where each colour is used, and a wider type scale. The main brand colours stayed. Lime was hard to use on interactive elements, so it became an accent and blue became the base. For charts, the client chose a theme toggle: colourful or all-blue.

## Delivery
**I agreed the delivery approach with the client.** Critical pages went to dev as soon as they were ready, not in one final batch. I sent a written structure and a task-level scope plan so the client could see dependencies and plan their developers' work. We cut deeper analysis of lower-priority report metrics.

- **Handoff batches** with clickable prototypes, acceptance criteria and edge cases (Home first, then Campaigns).
- **Design system:** I fixed only the components the redesign used, and documented the rest for later.
- **Final handoff:** prototype, all files moved to the client's Figma workspace, and a variables JSON export.
- In the last review round, **about 90% of client comments were closed.**

## What I learned
- **Set up the UX → UI transition in advance.** Client satisfaction dropped most at that point. They expected a fuller rebrand from the design system audit. Next time, I would agree what "audit" means at kick-off.
- **Build components only in the parent design system file.** Components created in working files lost their links when the files moved to the client's Figma workspace.
- **Next:** measure daily active clients, AI assistant usage and campaign-flow completion after release.

> **AI in my process:** I used Claude to keep a project "source of truth" from call transcripts, to write a structured brief for homepage drafts, and to map client comments to the AI assistant's screens and write copy for its 6 agents.
