## TL;DR
An AI advertising platform covered the full campaign cycle, but **users saw separate tools, not one product**. Reports sat in two places, "Create campaign" opened an unexpected planning tool, and the AI assistant went unnoticed. As UX lead, I ran an audit and 9 interviews, then rebuilt the product around **one Project entity, one guided campaign flow and an AI assistant inside the work**. We shipped it to developers in batches before a product presentation, and the client went on to work with our team on other projects.

## Context
The platform helps brands research audiences and competitors, plan media, launch campaigns and track results. It serves brand clients, internal managers and admins.

Constraints:
- **A full redesign close to the client's deadline.** Developers worked in parallel, so critical pages went to dev as soon as they were ready.
- A design system inherited from a previous agency, and a brand refresh at the same time.
- **Client role first.** Manager and admin flows were deferred.

*Client name, people and data are hidden under NDA.*

## The problem
**Business problem:** the client wanted a **full-cycle platform**, but the product looked like a tool for submitting campaigns, with research "on the side". In the VP of Product's words, they needed "a platform for researching, planning and creating campaigns".

**User problem:** users understood each tool but not the lifecycle. Navigation changed with the selected company or report, terms were inconsistent, and many users didn't know what the AI chat could do.

**How might we turn separate tools into one campaign workflow, with AI help where users already work?**

## Goals
- One entity and one flow from brief to reporting.
- Clear navigation: where users *do* things vs. where they *see* results.
- A homepage that is both a daily dashboard and a showcase of the full cycle.
- A consistent design system, so the product stops being built page by page.
- Main success signal: **by the end of month one, a new client checks the platform daily instead of waiting for an email report.**

## My role
- **Led the UX audit and 2 rounds of interviews:** 5 internal users, then 4 leads from Client Service, media planning (MPO) and growth.
- **Designed the core model:** lifecycle map, roles and access, entity tree, status map.
- **Defined IA, navigation and the campaign flow, and presented each stage** to the client's VP of Product and commercial team.
- **Designed the AI assistant:** draft user flow, UI, client presentation and revisions after review.
- **Built the estimate and the project timeline:** split the scope into tasks, set what runs in parallel and when each part goes to dev, so the client could plan their developers around it.
- Designed the homepage, Audience Personas and two Pro reports, and prepared the dev handoff batches. I worked with a design manager on planning and reviewed UI from 1–2 designers.

## Discovery & insights
I audited heuristics, consistency, analytics, flows and loading performance. **The audit logged 17 issues across 7 key screens: 1 blocker, 2 critical, 6 high and 8 medium.** Each one had a screenshot, a priority and its user impact. Then I mapped departments to modules and lifecycle stages from the interviews.

<Figure src="./images/connected-campaign-lifecycle-ai-platform/audit-top-issues.png" caption="Top findings, from a blocking save error to a 1.83:1 contrast ratio. They showed the problem went deeper than visuals." />

1. **Users saw tools, not a process.** In interviews, users explained each tool but not the lifecycle. → Build the product around a campaign entity.
2. **Reports lived in two places.** The audit found them in the main menu and inside campaigns. → Nest every report under its project.
3. **"Create campaign" broke the mental model.** It redirected to "Advanced Planning"; a client reviewer reported the same confusion. → Make planning a step inside creation.
4. **An empty dashboard looks broken.** Interviews showed data arrives on day 3 and is complete by day 7. → Onboarding explains the timeline instead of showing empty charts.
5. **AI value was invisible.** Users didn't know what the chat could do. → Put AI inside tasks, with ready-made actions.

## Exploring options
### One Project entity instead of separate features
I proposed a **Project** (formerly "Campaign") that exists **from the brief onward** and holds the media plan, research, reporting, activity log and files. I chose "Project" over "Campaign" because the entity has to exist before a campaign goes live. The client approved it as the base structure.

<Figure src="[TODO: entity-tree.png]" caption="Entity tree: staff → clients → projects. Each project holds its plan, research, reports, log and files." />

### One status, many views
The status map covers **12 statuses and 8 roles**. A project has one real status, but each role sees its own label. A client never sees an internal draft, and sees **"Needs Revision" instead of "Rejected"**.

<Figure src="[TODO: status-map.png]" caption="Status map: 12 statuses × 8 roles. One real state, different views per role." />

### Two modes, not a flat menu
The client's team proposed a flat menu: Campaigns, Research, Plan and AI at one level, like SEMrush. **I argued against it:** clients must easily tell where they *do* things from where they only *see* reports. The VP of Product agreed. **We kept two modes, Projects and Tools.** Tool results can be saved into a project or a client-level Brand Hub.

### Light sidebar over dark rail
I showed a dark icon rail and a light sidebar with a top bar. The commercial lead found the rail too heavy. **I reframed the choice:** we needed a clear hierarchy, not a dark colour. The merged option, a **light collapsible sidebar with search and profile in the header**, met every point of feedback.

<Figure src="[TODO: navigation-options.png]" caption="Option A (dark rail) vs. Option B (top header) for client, manager and admin. The merged option kept A's hierarchy and B's familiar utilities." />

## The solution
### 1. One button, one guided flow
**"Create New Campaign"** lives on the homepage and the Campaigns page. Advanced Planning is now step 1, not a separate tool. **Research is optional:** users can attach saved research or click "Continue without research". Unfinished plans save as drafts.

<Figure src="[TODO: campaign-flow-before-after.png]" caption="Before: 'Create campaign' redirected to a separate planning tool. After: one guided flow with an optional research step." />

### 2. An AI assistant inside the work
I drafted the flow and designed the assistant as a **persistent element, not a menu item**, with contextual entry points, a side panel that expands to full screen, and AI insights on the homepage. After the client review, I replaced the "Try asking" prompts with **quick-action tags that pre-fill an editable prompt**, and moved 6 specialised agents into a dropdown in the input field.

<Figure src="[TODO: ai-before-after.png]" caption="AI assistant before / after: a hidden chat vs. quick actions, an agent picker and contextual entry points." />

<Figure src="[TODO: ai-layouts.png]" caption="Side panel and full workspace: quick questions stay next to the task, longer analysis gets full space." />

### 3. A homepage with two jobs
The homepage is now a **daily dashboard and a full-cycle showcase**: Brand Hub, latest campaigns, tool widgets and AI insights. **Premium modules stay visible but locked, with a "Contact Us" button,** because upsell always goes through sales reps.

<Figure src="[TODO: homepage-before-after.png]" caption="Homepage before / after: from low-value overview to live data, saved research and AI insights." />

### 4. Onboarding that never starts empty
New clients arrive with one active campaign. We combined a short tour, a video knowledge base, contextual AI tips and a **day 3 / day 7 data timeline**. On day 3, the assistant offers to explain the first numbers.

<Figure src="[TODO: onboarding.png]" caption="Onboarding: tour, data timeline and the first AI explanation on day 3." />

### 5. Design system and brand
The audit found **contrast problems, an undefined palette structure and too few type styles**. I proposed a palette template that maps each colour to its use, and a wider type scale. Lime was hard to use on interactive elements, so it became an accent and blue the base. For charts, the client chose a colourful / all-blue theme toggle.

## Delivery
**I agreed the delivery approach with the client:** critical pages went to dev as soon as they were ready. I sent a task-level scope plan so the client could plan their developers around dependencies. We cut deeper analysis of lower-priority report metrics.

- **Handoff batches:** clickable prototypes, acceptance criteria and edge cases (Home first, then Campaigns).
- **Design system:** fixed only the components in use, documented the rest.
- **Final handoff:** prototype, files moved to the client's Figma workspace, variables JSON export.
- **About 90% of client comments** were closed in the last review round.

<Figure src="[TODO: design-system.png]" caption="Updated components and the palette template: each colour mapped to where it is used." />

## Impact
- **The client approved the Project entity, two-mode navigation and the guided campaign flow** as the new base of the product. Their commercial lead called the Projects vs Tools structure an upgrade.
- **The full design scope after the audit took ≈1 month of task time against a ≈2-month estimate** (internal retrospective). We added a second designer mid-project to make this possible.
- The client later paused the next phase because their development timeline moved. **Based on this project, they continued working with our team on their other products.**
- Impact on adoption, retention and AI usage was not measured.

> "I really like how you kind of integrated the interactions with [the AI assistant] from time to time." — Project coordinator, client side

## What I learned
- **Set up the UX → UI transition in advance.** Client satisfaction dropped most at that point; they expected a fuller rebrand from the design system audit. Next time, I would agree what "audit" means at kick-off.
- **Build components only in the parent design system file.** Components made in working files lost their links when files moved to the client's workspace.
- **Next:** measure daily active clients, AI assistant usage and campaign-flow completion.

> **AI in my process:** I used my own Claude skills to draft user flows, the customer journey map, and the role and status matrices from interview notes. This gave me a first version faster. The entity model, the status rules and the navigation decisions stayed with me and the client.
