## Description
The platform lets **anyone who knows about a property for sale send it as a lead** to a real estate agent and get a reward if it sells. One lead passes through three roles: Contributors, Managers and Agents. I designed **the logic behind that journey** (who sees what, when a lead moves, what stops it) and then **the app for contributors and agents on mobile, tablet and web**. While mapping the flow, **I found that nobody had defined how a lead reaches an agent**, and worked out the model with the client. **The app launched and passed 1,000+ downloads on Google Play.**

## Context
The product is a **referral platform, not a listing app**. Contributors submit property leads, track them and earn a reward on a sale. Managers review leads. Agents take approved leads and sell the property. *In the app, contributors are called Tipsters and agents are called Brokers.*

- **An early-stage startup.** Limited budget and team, so **no complex third-party integrations** at the start.
- **Third-party data.** Every lead comes from someone who is not the owner, so it has to be checked.

## The problem
**Business problem:** the business earns only if leads turn into sales. **A lead that gets stuck, is fake, or reaches the wrong agent** costs the contributor's trust and the agent's time.

**User problem:** three roles touch the same lead but need different things. **A contributor wants to know the lead is moving. A manager needs to know what to review. An agent should only see leads that are ready to work on.**

**How might we move one property lead through three roles so that each one always knows its next step, without exposing internal workflow?**

## Goals
- **One shared model** for roles, permissions and statuses, used by the client and developers.
- **A clear status for every role** at every stage of the lead.
- A verification process that **makes each responsibility explicit**.
- A workflow the startup can run **without heavy automation**.

1. **Same lead, different questions.** The contributor asks "Is it moving?", the manager "Do I need to act?", the agent "Can I take it?". → **Role-specific statuses instead of one shared list.**
2. **Leads come from people who are not the owner.** → **Verification with owner consent** before an agent gets the lead.
3. **The flow had a gap between "approved" and "agent".** No rule said who picks the agent. → **An assignment model, agreed with the client.**
4. **Not every rejection means the same thing.** A wrong agent, a fake property and a missing consent need different outcomes. → **Separate exception paths in the status map.**

## Exploring options
### Role-specific statuses over one shared status
One status list for everyone would be simpler to build, but it would **show agents leads they cannot use yet and show contributors internal steps**. **I chose to separate the internal status from the label each role sees.** Right after a contributor submits a lead:

- **Contributor** sees **"Under Review"**: the lead was received.
- **Manager** sees **"Pending Review"**: it needs their action.
- **Agent** sees nothing: the lead has not passed review yet.

<Figure src="[TODO: status-map.png]" caption="Status map: one internal status, three role labels, and the actions that move a lead to the next stage." />

### Manual status updates over integrations
A fully automated flow with listing-service and CRM integrations **was not realistic for an early-stage startup**. **I designed the process around manual updates**: agents update the status of their leads, and managers **get an alert when a status has not changed for too long**. This kept the first version buildable and split the work clearly: **agents handle the property, managers watch the process**.

### Who picks the agent
While I mapped the flow, **I saw that the system did not say how an approved lead reaches an agent**. I raised it with the client, and **we agreed on a model**: the contributor chooses the agent, each agent sets their own referral reward percentage, and the chosen agent gets the lead. **We also discussed assigning an agent for the contributor.** Letting the contributor choose won, because it works better for both sides: **contributors can compare agents by their reviews**, and **agents can compete with different reward rates**. The reward is clear before the contributor sends the lead.


### Visual direction
**The design lead explored the visual direction** (moodboards and two visual identity options) and set the core UI style. **I designed all the screens and components on top of it**, so my part here was keeping the new style consistent across mobile, tablet and web.

## The solution
### 1. Verification in three checkpoints
- **Cross-source check.** The lead is compared with other listing services. **A match approves it automatically** at this step.
- **Owner consent.** A request goes to the owner's contact. If the owner declines or does not answer, **the contributor is asked to contact the owner or fix the contact details**. Without consent, the lead does not move.
- **On-site check.** After assignment, **the agent visits the property** and confirms the data.

<Figure src="[TODO: verification-flow.png]" caption="Verification flow: cross-source check, owner consent and on-site visit, with the person responsible for each step." />

### 2. Edge cases with clear outcomes
- **Agent mismatch:** the property is outside the agent's specialisation → **back to reassignment**.
- **Fake or invalid property** → **cancelled**, not reassigned.
- **No owner consent** → **blocked** until the consent issue is solved.
- **Not found on listing services** → **manual review stayed an open product decision**.

<Figure src="[TODO: edge-cases.png]" caption="Exception paths in the status map: reassign, cancel, block or review, so a rejection always has a defined next step." />

### 3. A contributor app that answers "Is it moving?"
- **Choosing a broker.** Each broker card shows a rating, badges (Top Seller, Verified, years of experience) and **"Your Potential Reward"**. The public profile adds reviews, so **the contributor compares brokers before sending the lead**.
- **Submitting a lead in three steps:** property info, seller contact, then **Review & Sign the Agreement** with the payout stated up front: "If this deal closes, you will receive 220 €".
- **The dashboard** shows the payout balance and every tip with **a status chip and a filter by status**. Tips that need the contributor's action get **"Action required" with a countdown**.
- **A rejected tip explains itself:** the reason ("Owner has no intention to sell"), a short note, and a **"Refer New Property"** button, so a rejection does not end the journey.

<Figure src="./images/three-roles-one-property-lead/contributor-tracking.png" caption="Property tips with status chips and a countdown on the one that needs action, and a rejected tip with its reason and a next step." />

### 4. An agent app built around status updates
- **Leads list** with status chips and **a timer on every new lead**, so the broker knows how long they have to respond.
- **New lead details** show the estimated value and commission, but **the owner's contacts stay locked until the broker accepts the lead**.
- **Rejecting needs a reason:** "Not operating in this region", "Not working with this property type" or "Other". **These reasons feed the agent-mismatch path**, where the lead goes back for reassignment.
- **After contacting the owner**, the broker confirms or corrects the estimated value and commission. **The change is shown on the lead** (for example +4,200 €), and every status update lands on the lead's timeline, up to **Mark as Sold**.

### 5. The same flows on tablet and web
Both roles also got **a tablet layout (1024) and a web app (1440)** with the same statuses and lead logic.

- **Web:** a sidebar for navigation and **leads as a table** with the same status chips and timers as on mobile. The broker's sidebar has Analytics, Finances, Team and Commission; the contributor's has Wallet, Brokers and Affiliate Program.
- **Lead details on web** put **the timeline in a side column**, so the broker sees the lead, the contacts and every status change on one screen.
- **Tablet:** landscape layouts that reuse the mobile components, with pop-ups such as the payout option centred on the screen.

- **Status is a message, not a field.** The same state needs different words for each role.
